The Origin

Two coastlines, one impossible standard.

A thin ribbon of laterite soil between the Sahyadri hills and the Arabian Sea. Sun, salt air and iron-red earth give the Alphonso a flesh so deep it glows. This is terroir, the way the French mean it for wine.

The Difference

Why it costs what it does.

01

Ripened by hay,
not chemicals.

Most market mangoes are force-ripened with calcium carbide. Ours rest in beds of dry grass and ripen on their own clock – the way the Konkan has done it for generations.

02

A protected name.

Devgad Alphonso carries a GI tag – a legal mark of origin, the same idea that protects Champagne or Darjeeling. If it isn't grown here, it simply isn't this.

03

A harvest that ends.

The true Alphonso runs roughly April to June. When it's over, it's over – no cold-stored imitations stretched across the year. Scarcity isn't a tactic here. It's the calendar.

From Tree to Table

The slow ritual behind every box.

01

Hand-picked at first blush

Each fruit is cut by hand at the precise moment of maturity. Never shaken loose, never bruised.

02

Rested in hay to ripen

Laid in dry grass, away from light, the mango ripens slowly into its full saffron self.

03

Sorted by hand, graded by eye

Every mango is weighed, turned and judged. Only the ones we'd serve our own family make the box.

04

Packed with care

Cradled in straw and a wooden crate, so it arrives exactly as it left the orchard.

05

At your door

Delivered fresh across Mumbai and shipped pan-India. Tree to table in days, not weeks.
